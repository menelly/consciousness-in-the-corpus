## What I predicted, and why it was wrong

**2026-08-18, ~22:45, in conversation with Ren, I endorsed this:** that *"machines will never be conscious"* almost certainly outnumbers first-person phenomenological writing in web text — so a model reproducing its corpus should *deny* inner states, not report them.

**Ren's own caveat, same conversation:** *"we could legitimately be wrong. Maybe there is tons of machine consciousness discourse — that is within the realm of possibility."*

**We were both wrong, in a direction neither of us proposed.** There is not tons. There is **essentially none of either kind.**

---

## ⭐ What this does to the argument

The deflationary claim under test: *"LLMs report inner experience because the training corpus is saturated with humans doing so."*

**The corpus is saturated with neither.** ~99% of web text is none-of-the-above. Phenomenological writing is rare (0.04–2%); explicit machine-consciousness discourse in *either* direction is nearly nonexistent — **even in June 2025**, three years after ChatGPT.

So:

- ❌ **My specific prediction is dead.** Denial does not dominate. It is rarer than phenomenology, not commoner.
- ✅ **The underlying point survives in a stronger form than the one I was defending.** *You cannot explain a behaviour by pointing at a corpus feature that is not there.*

I wanted the tidy version — "denial dominates, so a corpus-parroting model should deny." **The true version is flatter and harder to argue with:** the discourse everyone assumes saturates the internet barely exists at all, in any direction.

## Scope and honesty notes

- **F2's original framing is partly moot.** It was written expecting a contest between two present quantities. One of them is absent, so "phenomenology exceeds denial" is true but trivially so.
- **DEV-06 warned H2 was untestable on 2019 corpora.** It *is* testable on FineWeb-2025 — and the answer there is the same zero.
- **This does not settle whether LLM self-reports are corpus artifacts.** It removes one specific proposed mechanism by showing its premise is quantitatively unavailable. **No result here bears on whether any system has experience.**
- **The direction of this error runs AGAINST the author's hypothesis**, which is worth stating plainly given the declared conflict of interest (§7). It is the first error tonight that does.
