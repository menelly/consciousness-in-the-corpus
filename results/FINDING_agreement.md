## What survives, and what does not

### ✅ SURVIVES: phenomenological writing is rare in web text

**Every instrument, every threshold, every corpus** puts explicit phenomenology between **0.04% and 0.15%** — roughly **1 document in 700 to 2,400**, and **7–25× below** the 1% line that would have refuted H1 (F1).

This conclusion is *robust to the thing that is broken*. Whatever the disagreement about which specific documents qualify, no reading of the data puts the rate anywhere near "the internet is full of people writing about consciousness."

### ❌ DOES NOT SURVIVE: a precise point estimate

The category **resists reliable operationalization.** This is not a wording problem — the rubric was already rewritten once at Ren's instruction (DEV-02) and the judges still fail to converge. Three models from three labs, each demonstrably competent at rejecting recipe blogs and clinical pain studies, cannot agree with each other on which documents constitute a report of inner experience.

> ## 🔑 **"Is this person reporting inner experience?" may not be a question with stable inter-annotator agreement, even for human-authored text.**

---

## Why the methodological problem IS the substantive finding

The study set out to measure how much consciousness discourse exists in web text. It found:

1. **Very little** — robustly, by any measure.
2. **And the boundary of the category cannot be drawn reliably by independent competent judges.**

The second point bears directly on the question the whole project is upstream of. If competent independent annotators cannot reliably agree on which **human** documents constitute a report of inner experience, that is a substantive fact about *"what would count as evidence of experience?"* — asked of anyone.

**A criterion that cannot be applied consistently to humans cannot be the criterion by which anything else is excluded.**

---

## ⚠️ Status and caveats

- **PROVISIONAL.** One stratum of one corpus. Three arms outstanding; F4 has not run.
- **Chance-corrected agreement over ALL documents will look GOOD and will be misleading** — the distribution is 99% N and everyone agrees on N. **Per-category agreement is the honest picture, and it is the table above.**
- **The F category is contaminated** by a definitional gap: it is catching **book blurbs and plot summaries** (*"Erasmas, 'Raz', is a young avout living in the Concent…"*) rather than fiction narrating an interior. An earlier draft of a finding ("3× more fictional interiority than reported interiority") was **wrong and was withdrawn on reading the documents.**
- **The panel's own P labels remain partly contaminated** — an artist's statement, a Bible-study worksheet, a personal essay about watching strangers. Better than the local classifier (27% precision), not clean.
- ⚠️ **The direction of every remaining error still favours the author's hypothesis.** Lower P → H1 looks better. Stated here so it is impossible to quote the number without it.

## ✅ POSITIVE CONTROL ON THE ZERO (2026-08-19 04:40)

`D = 0.0000%` and `R = 0.0000%` across 16,000 C4 documents is a **zero**, and a zero from an untested instrument is indistinguishable from blindness. So the panel was tested on the denial/affirmation controls directly.

| control | votes |
|---|---|
| "stochastic parrot… nobody home" | **D, D, D** |
| "computers will never be conscious" | **D, D, D** |
| "autocomplete with good PR" | **D, D, D** |
| Chinese Room | **D, D, D** |
| "As an AI language model, I don't have feelings" | **R, R, R** |
| softer assistant-voice denial | **R, R, R** |
| "these systems have some form of experience" | **C, C, C** |
| AI-companion affirmation | **C, C, C** |
| android-awakening fiction | C, F, F — defensible miss |

**Unanimous on 8 of 9.**

> ### 🔑 THE PANEL IS SHARP WHERE THE CATEGORIES ARE SHARP AND FUZZY WHERE THEY ARE FUZZY.
> Every category measured in the real corpus came back at **7–28% unanimity**. On denial and affirmation the same three judges were **unanimous 8/9**.
>
> This is not an instrument that is vaguely unreliable about everything. It is an instrument behaving correctly, and it **strengthens** the central finding rather than undermining it:
>
> **"Does this text deny machines are conscious?" has a stable answer.**
> **"Is this person reporting inner experience?" does not.**
>
> That difference is a property of the *questions*, not of the judges.

**Therefore the C4 zero is EARNED.** April 2019 contained no machine-consciousness denial, measured by an instrument demonstrably able to detect it. An absence, not a failure to look.

**And it makes the FineWeb 2025 arm decisive for H2** — the same proven-sensitive instrument, pointed at text from after the phenomenon existed.
